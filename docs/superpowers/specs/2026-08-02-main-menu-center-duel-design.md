# Main-Menu Center Duel Design

## Goal

Replace the two isolated swordsmen in
`res://pics/main_menu_background_phone.png` with a lively martial gathering:
two swordsmen actively fence on the center slab while twelve to sixteen people
watch from the eight surrounding slabs.

## Central Duel

The two swordsmen occupy only the center slab. Their feet remain comfortably
inside it and their opposing poses form one immediately readable exchange of
blades. Both bodies, weapons, and movement clearly respond to one another. The
action is energetic but compact enough that neither fighter, robe, nor sword
touches a seam or enters a neighboring slab.

## Spectators

Distribute twelve to sixteen distinct martial-world spectators unevenly across
all eight surrounding slabs. Use a loose ring rather than one rigid figure per
cell: some people stand alone, while others form small two- or three-person
clusters. Vary age, clothing, posture, hairstyle, and carried equipment so the
crowd feels incidental rather than duplicated.

Every spectator looks toward or reacts to the center duel. Keep each complete
silhouette inside its assigned surrounding slab, away from seams and the center
slab. The duel remains the dominant focal point at phone size.

## Preservation Boundary

Retain the existing Chinese ink-wash medium, restrained muted palette, arena
perspective, nine slabs, railings, towers, ribbons, surrounding mountains and
mist, large blank title area, portrait composition, and image dimensions. Do
not add text, UI, banners, extra architecture, or figures outside the arena.

## Acceptance

At normal mobile scale, the viewer should first read two swordsmen fencing in
the center, then notice a varied crowd watching from every surrounding slab.
No person or weapon crosses a grid seam, and the audience does not obscure the
central action.
